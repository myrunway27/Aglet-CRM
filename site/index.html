Built for the climate

90% humidity is the real test

South Florida summers sit at 75–90% relative humidity. Particleboard boxes swell, cheap veneers lift, and doors that fit in January bind in August. We build against that, every time:

  • Columbia Forest hardwood plywood boxes — never particleboard, never MDF carcasses.
  • Blum soft-close hinges and undermount runners, lifetime-warranted.
  • Species chosen for stability — white oak, maple and sapele move far less here than cherry or hickory.
  • Conversion-varnish and low-VOC finishes sprayed in our own booth, cured before they ship.

Before you call

Questions we get every week

How much do custom kitchen cabinets cost in Fort Lauderdale?

A same-footprint refit generally runs $650–$900 per linear foot installed; a fully custom kitchen with an island and integrated appliance panels runs $900–$1,400. South Florida sits roughly 15–25% above Central Florida for the same scope. We give a fixed number after the measure — not an allowance that drifts.

How long does a custom kitchen take?

Four to eight weeks from approved design to installed, depending on finish and size. Dealers quoting imported cabinetry are typically at eight to sixteen weeks, because their lead time includes freight.

Do you actually build the cabinets, or resell them?

We build them. Delta has operated its own mill in South Florida for over 25 years — we design, cut, assemble, sand and spray in-house, and nothing is imported. You are welcome to walk the shop floor and watch your own kitchen being made.

What plywood and hardware do you use?

Columbia Forest hardwood plywood carcasses — never particleboard — with Blum soft-close hinges and undermount drawer runners. In a climate that runs 75–90% humidity all summer, the box material is what determines whether a kitchen still closes properly in ten years.

Do you work with contractors and designers?

Constantly. We sequence around your GC's schedule, provide shop drawings for permitting where required, and handle commercial casework as well as residential.

Which wood species hold up best in Florida?

White oak, maple and sapele are the most dimensionally stable choices for this climate. Cherry and hickory move considerably more with seasonal humidity swings, so we'll talk you out of them for a coastal house unless the look is non-negotiable.
